# Native Mushroom Species in the Mideast Region

In the Mideast region, several native mushroom species are likely to be found in the wild and on debris. Here are a few notable examples:

## 1. Agaricus bisporus (Common Button Mushroom)
- **Identification**: Look for small to medium-sized mushrooms with a smooth, white to light brown cap. The gills are free and white, turning brown as they mature.
- **Habitat**: Commonly found in grassy areas and on decaying organic matter.
- **Liquid Culture Formula**: 
  - 500 mL water
  - 10 g light malt extract
  - 5 g dextrose
  - 2 g yeast extract
- **Ease of Positive ID**: High; commonly known and easily recognizable.
- **Candidacy for Growing**: Excellent for cultivation; widely available in stores.

## 2. Pleurotus ostreatus (Oyster Mushroom)
- **Identification**: Characterized by its oyster-shaped cap, which can be white, gray, or yellowish. The gills are white and run down the stem.
- **Habitat**: Commonly found growing on decaying wood and adaptable to various substrates, including debris.
- **Liquid Culture Formula**: 
  - 500 mL water
  - 20 g light malt extract
  - 5 g dextrose
- **Ease of Positive ID**: High; distinctive shape and habitat.
- **Candidacy for Growing**: Excellent; can be cultivated on various substrates.

## 3. Lactarius deliciosus (Saffron Milk Cap)
- **Identification**: Features a reddish-orange cap with a slightly sticky surface. When cut, it exudes a distinctive orange milk.
- **Habitat**: Typically grows in coniferous forests, often associated with pine trees.
- **Liquid Culture Formula**: 
  - 500 mL water
  - 15 g light malt extract
  - 5 g dextrose
- **Ease of Positive ID**: Moderate; requires knowledge of milk caps.
- **Candidacy for Growing**: Moderate; more challenging to cultivate.

## 4. Morchella esculenta (Morel)
- **Identification**: Recognizable by its honeycomb appearance with a conical cap. The stem is hollow.
- **Habitat**: Often found in disturbed areas, such as those with debris from fallen trees.
- **Liquid Culture Formula**: 
  - 500 mL water
  - 20 g light malt extract
  - 5 g dextrose
- **Ease of Positive ID**: High; unique appearance.
- **Candidacy for Growing**: Challenging; more difficult to cultivate than other species.

## 5. Trametes versicolor (Turkey Tail)
- **Identification**: This polypore mushroom has a fan-shaped cap with concentric zones of color, often in shades of brown, white, and gray.
- **Habitat**: Commonly found on decaying wood and recognized for its medicinal properties.
- **Liquid Culture Formula**: 
  - 500 mL water
  - 15 g light malt extract
  - 5 g dextrose
- **Ease of Positive ID**: High; easily identifiable by its coloration and growth pattern.
- **Candidacy for Growing**: Good; can be cultivated for medicinal use.

## 6. Ganoderma lucidum (Reishi)
- **Identification**: Recognizable by its shiny, reddish-brown cap and white to yellowish underside. It has a kidney-shaped or fan-like appearance.
- **Habitat**: Often found on hardwoods, particularly on decaying logs.
- **Liquid Culture Formula**: 
  - 500 mL water
  - 20 g light malt extract
  - 5 g dextrose
- **Ease of Positive ID**: High; distinctive appearance and habitat.
- **Candidacy for Growing**: Good; valued for its health benefits.

These species not only contribute to the biodiversity of the region but also hold cultural and culinary significance. When foraging, always ensure proper identification and consult local guidelines to promote sustainable practices.
