## Optimized Plan for Mycelial Expansion Using a Liquid Loop

### Materials Needed

- **Liquid Culture**: Mycelium grown in a nutrient-rich liquid medium.
- **Peptone**: Optional, for enhanced growth.
- **Nutrient Medium**: A mixture of water, light malt extract, dextrose, and peptone.
- **Improvised Diastolic Pump**: A simple peristaltic pump or a DIY diaphragm pump for circulation and aeration.
- **Sterile Containers**: For the liquid culture and mycelial growth.
- **Air Filter**: To ensure sterile air enters the system.
- **Sterilization Equipment**: Pressure cooker or autoclave.
- **Tubing**: Sterile, food-grade tubing for the liquid loop.
- **Sterile Environment**: A clean workspace to minimize contamination.
- **pH Meter**: For accurate pH adjustments.
- **Temperature Control System**: To maintain optimal growth conditions.

---

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Prepare the Nutrient Medium:
1. Mix the following in a clean container:
   - **500 mL water**
   - **10 g light malt extract**
   - **5 g dextrose**
   - **1-2 g peptone (optional)**
2. Adjust the pH to around **5.5-6.5** for optimal growth using a pH meter.

#### Sterilize the Nutrient Medium:
1. Pour the nutrient medium into sterile containers.
2. Sterilize using a pressure cooker at **15 psi for 30-60 minutes**.
3. Allow the containers to cool in a sterile environment.

#### Set Up the Liquid Loop:
1. Connect the sterile containers to the improvised diastolic pump using sterile tubing.
2. Ensure that the pump creates a continuous flow without breaking the sterile environment.
3. Install an air filter at the inlet of the system to allow for aeration while preventing contamination.

#### Inoculate the Liquid Culture:
1. Once the nutrient medium has cooled, use sterile techniques to inoculate it with mycelium from a previous culture.
2. Ensure even distribution of the mycelium in the nutrient medium.

#### Activate the Pump:
1. Start the improvised diastolic pump to circulate the liquid culture.
2. This circulation will provide aeration and nutrients to the mycelium, promoting faster growth.

#### Monitor Growth:
1. Keep the system in a warm, dark environment (ideally around **70-80°F or 21-27°C**).
2. Regularly check for signs of contamination and ensure the air filter is functioning properly.

#### Harvesting Mycelium:
1. As the mycelium grows, periodically harvest a portion of the liquid culture through the pump system.
2. Use a sterile container to collect the harvested mycelium, ensuring that the remaining culture continues to thrive.

#### Continuous Expansion:
1. To expand the culture, add fresh nutrient medium to the system while maintaining the flow.
2. Connect additional sterile containers of nutrient medium to the loop, allowing for continuous growth without breaking the sterile environment.

#### Maintain Sterility:
1. Regularly check all connections, tubing, and containers for signs of contamination.
2. Replace the air filter as needed to ensure a continuous supply of sterile air.

---

### Additional Considerations

- **Pump Design**: Ensure the improvised diastolic pump is made from materials that can withstand sterilization and are non-toxic to the mycelium.
- **Nutrient Adjustments**: Monitor the growth rate and adjust the nutrient concentrations as needed to optimize expansion.
- **Temperature Control**: Consider using a temperature-controlled environment to maintain optimal growth conditions.
- **Contamination Prevention**: Implement a UV sterilization step for the air entering the system to further reduce contamination risks.
- **Monitoring System**: Use sensors to monitor pH and temperature continuously, allowing for real-time adjustments to maintain optimal growth conditions.

By implementing these improvements, you can achieve maximum mycelial expansion while maintaining a sterile environment, allowing for efficient harvesting and continuous growth.
