Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA) and Malt Extract Agar (MEA) are both general fungal media, but they differ in formulation and typical uses:

Carbon source and nutrients:
SDA: high dextrose (glucose) content and peptone; favors many pathogenic and clinical yeasts and molds, especially when acidic pH is used to suppress bacteria.
MEA: malt extract provides complex carbohydrates and nutrients that favor environmental and food-related fungi, especially fast-growing molds and many basidiomycetes (mushroom mycelia).
Typical uses where SDA is preferred over MEA:
Isolation and cultivation of dermatophytes, clinical yeasts (Candida), and other pathogenic fungi in clinical/mycology labs because SDA’s composition and often lower pH (≈5.6) inhibit bacterial growth.
When you want to suppress bacterial contaminants while still supporting fungal growth (SDA’s higher glucose + peptone and acidic pH make it selective for fungi).
Routine culturing for identification of yeasts and some slower-growing pathogenic molds.
MEA strengths where SDA may be less ideal:
Supporting robust growth, sporulation, and pigmentation of many environmental and basidiomycete fungi (e.g., Pleurotus, Ganoderma) used in food/mycology work.
Encouraging typical colony morphology and sporulation useful for environmental isolates.
Practical takeaway:

Use SDA when you need a fungus-selective medium (especially for yeasts/pathogens) or when bacterial suppression is important.
Use MEA (or PDA) for culturing environmental mushroom strains and for promoting natural colony morphology and sporulation.
