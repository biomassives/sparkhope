Creating your own Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA) from homegrown potatoes is not only cost-effective but also ensures you know exactly what nutrients are going into your growing medium. This wholistic approach connects your mycology work directly to your garden.

## Why Make Your Own PDA?

- **Food Security**: Reduce dependency on commercial suppliers
- **Quality Control**: Know exactly what's in your medium
- **Cost Effective**: Much cheaper than pre-made media
- **Sustainability**: Use your own homegrown resources
- **Better Nutrition**: Fresh potatoes often have superior nutrient profiles

## Ingredients Needed

### Primary Components
- **200g fresh potatoes** (peeled and diced) - any variety works, waxy potatoes preferred
- **20g dextrose** (or glucose powder)
- **15-20g agar powder** (15g for softer medium, 20g for firmer)
- **1000ml distilled water**

### Optional Additions
- **1-2g yeast extract** - for enhanced nitrogen content
- **pH adjustment strips** - to monitor acidity levels

## Equipment Required

- Large pot for boiling
- Fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth
- Measuring cups and scale
- Pressure cooker or autoclave
- Sterile petri dishes
- Still air box or laminar flow hood (recommended)

## Step-by-Step Process

### 1. Prepare Potato Extract

1. **Clean and prepare potatoes**: Wash thoroughly, peel, and dice into 1-2cm cubes
2. **Boil potatoes**: Add diced potatoes to 500ml distilled water in a large pot
3. **Cook thoroughly**: Boil for 15-20 minutes until potatoes are very soft and breaking apart
4. **Extract nutrients**: Strain through cheesecloth or fine mesh, pressing the pulp to extract maximum liquid
5. **Measure extract**: You should have approximately 400ml of potato broth (add distilled water if short)

### 2. Create the Medium

1. **Dissolve dextrose**: Add 20g dextrose to the warm potato broth, stir until completely dissolved
2. **Add remaining water**: Pour in the remaining 600ml distilled water
3. **Incorporate agar**: Slowly sprinkle agar powder while stirring continuously to prevent clumping
4. **Optional boost**: Add 1-2g yeast extract if desired for enhanced growth

### 3. Sterilization Process

#### Autoclave Method (Preferred)
- Sterilize at **15 PSI (121°C) for 15 minutes**
- Allow natural pressure release

#### Pressure Cooker Method
- Cook at high pressure for **20 minutes**
- Allow natural pressure release

### 4. Pouring Plates

1. **Cool properly**: Let medium cool to 50-60°C (warm to touch, not hot)
2. **Work sterile**: Pour in still air box or laminar flow hood
3. **Fill plates**: Pour approximately 20ml per 90mm petri dish
4. **Cool completely**: Allow plates to solidify at room temperature
5. **Store properly**: Refrigerate in sealed bags until use

## Pro Tips for Success

### Potato Selection
- **Waxy varieties** (like Yukon Gold) provide better starch content
- **Fresh is best** - use recently harvested potatoes when possible
- **Avoid green potatoes** - these contain solanine which can inhibit growth

### Clarity Improvement
- **Double strain** through coffee filters for clearer broth
- **Skim foam** during boiling to reduce cloudiness
- **Let settle** before final straining

### pH Considerations
- Natural potato pH (around 5.6) works well for most applications
- Test with pH strips if working with sensitive organisms
- Adjust with small amounts of lemon juice (lower) or baking soda (raise) if needed

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### Cloudy Agar
- **Cause**: Inadequate straining or too much potato starch
- **Solution**: Filter through coffee filters, use less potato material

### Soft Agar
- **Cause**: Insufficient agar powder or pH too low
- **Solution**: Increase agar to 18-20g per liter

### Poor Growth
- **Cause**: Over-sterilization or nutrient depletion
- **Solution**: Reduce sterilization time, add yeast extract

## Storage and Shelf Life

- **Prepared plates**: 2-3 weeks refrigerated in sealed bags
- **Unused medium**: Can be re-melted and re-sterilized once
- **Signs of contamination**: Discard if you see unusual colors or smells

## Cost Comparison

Making your own PDA costs approximately **$0.15-0.25 per plate** compared to **$0.75-1.50** for commercial pre-made plates, representing significant savings for active mycologists.

## Next Steps

Once you've mastered basic PDA, consider experimenting with:
- Different potato varieties for varying nutrient profiles
- Adding other garden vegetables for custom media
- Adjusting dextrose concentrations for specific applications
- Creating specialized media for different fungal species
